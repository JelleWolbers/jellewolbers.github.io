Backups for Everyone

Jelle Wolbers — 2026

Many years ago my backup hard drive suddenly failed and I lost many photos, videos, songs that I'll never get back. Since then I've been researching different solutions to become better at keeping my data safe. In this blogpost I'll show you different ways to do this so you can pick one that is robust enough for you and fits the effort and money you want to invest.

Ways to Store and Backup Your Data

  • On your phone
  • On your computer
  • On an external drive (HDD or SSD)
  • On a family member or friends computer
  • On a cloud service such as iCloud, Google Drive/Photos, OneDrive, Dropbox, BackBlaze etc.
  • On a NAS in your house (NAS stands for Networked Attached Storage, which is basically a box with hard drives and control software that is placed somewhere in your house and can be accessed from any device in your house. Besides shared folders for storage, a NAS can also run software such as automatically backing up its data to a remote system. More powerful NAS devices can also serve movies and TV shows to your TV(s), kind of like a local Netflix.)
  • On a remote NAS (e.g. at a family member or friends house) or any combination of the above.
  • On two external drives, one in your house and one elsewhere
  • On the integrated cloud service and two hard drives, one in your house and one elsewhere
  • On a NAS at home + external drive elsewehre
  • on a cloud service + nas at home + sexternal drive elsewhere

This is not an exhaustive list so feel free to combine any of these but these examples should give you a basic idea of the trade-offs

Specific backup systems

The 3-2-1 system

If you read about how to do backups you'll find many people recommending the 3-2-1 system (keep 3 copies of your data, stored on 2 different media types (e.g. SSD, cloud), of which 1 is located off-site). This is a great system but also costs a lot of time or money to set-up and maintain.

Conclusion

If things in your life change, such as the importance of your data (e.g. you're keeping more photos, or that of your partner/children as well) and therefore your preferred robustness, the money you have or the time you're willing to invest, you can always change your system. The important thing is to pick a strategy that works for you at this time. So pick something that you will probably do and start with that. For most people advice is: online system is fine for daily use but make sure to periodically make back-ups to store locally.

Other stuff
